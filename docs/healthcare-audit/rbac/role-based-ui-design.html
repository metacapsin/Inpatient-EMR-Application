Inpatient EMR · Role-based access design · For client review

Every department sees its own hospital

One login currently shows every screen to every member of staff. This proposal splits the system into department portals: a lab technician opens a lab worklist, a nurse opens her unit, a billing officer opens revenue. Requests flow between departments and come back as reviewed results.

1 · The principle

Three levels of access, one rule

A member of staff only sees the screens their job needs. The rule is enforced on the server, not just hidden in the interface, so a hidden screen cannot be reached by typing its address.

Full
View and act

The screen is in the menu and every action on it is available to this role. Example: a lab technician on the lab worklist.

View
Read only

The screen is in the menu, but buttons that change the record are absent. Example: a pharmacist reading a patient's lab results.

Hidden
Not available

The screen is not in the menu and the server refuses the request. Example: a billing officer and clinical notes.

Clinical only
Licensed actions

Signing notes, administering medication, acknowledging critical results and finalising discharge are reserved for licensed clinical roles. The administrator can see these screens but cannot perform these actions.

5 · Cross-department workflow

A lab request, from the ward to the lab and back

Ward staff ask for a test, the laboratory performs it and uploads the report, the ward reviews the result. Each department only ever sees its own step. The same seven-step pattern is reused for imaging, medical records and pharmacy.

Order status:ordered→collected→in progress→preliminary→final→reviewed / acknowledged
already in the systemnew in this workautomatic, no person involved

The same pattern, three more times

ImagingPhysician orders a study → radiology technologist performs it and uploads the report → radiologist finalises → physician reviews.
Medical recordsNurse or physician requests outside records → medical-records clerk scans and uploads → requester is notified and the file appears in the chart.
PharmacyPhysician prescribes → pharmacist verifies or rejects in the pharmacy queue → nurse sees the verified medication on the administration record.
HousekeepingNurse or bed manager requests a bed clean → housekeeping accepts and completes → bed board shows the bed as ready.
6 · Rollout

Four phases, about six weeks

Phase A is the foundation everything else stands on. Phases B and C can run in parallel with two developers.

Phase A · Week 1

Permission foundation

  • Named permissions behind every screen and action
  • New roles added to user management
  • Menus, pages and chart tabs filter by role
  • Each role lands on its own home page
Phase B · Weeks 2–3

Department portals

  • Provider, nursing, lab, imaging, records, front-desk, pharmacy and revenue worklists
  • One shared worklist design: status tabs, patient and bed, age of request, assign to me
  • Hospital dashboard kept for administrators
Phase C · Weeks 4–5

Request → fulfil → review

  • Lab report upload, verify step, results inbox
  • Imaging report upload and radiologist sign-off
  • Medical-records request queue
  • Notification to the ordering clinician on every final result
Phase D · Week 6

Hardening

  • Hospital can switch individual permissions on or off per role
  • Nurses default to their own unit's patients
  • Every refused access is written to the audit trail
  • Unacknowledged critical results escalate to the charge nurse, then the attending
7 · Decisions for the hospital

Four questions that change the build

Is a radiologist a separate role, or a physician with a specialty?Proposal assumes a specialty flag on the physician role. A separate role adds one column to the matrix and one landing page.
Should lab results be verified before clinicians see them?Proposal assumes results entered by a technician stay preliminary until verified; results arriving from a laboratory system later release immediately.
Should a nurse see only her own unit's patients at launch?Unit scoping is built in Phase D. A small facility may prefer to see all inpatients and skip it.
Do housekeeping, quality and allied health need logins in the first release?Their screens exist with limited depth. They can be added as roles later without touching the other portals.
